    RETAKE / DOCS / WATCH MODE

    Watch an AI
    test your app.

    Watch mode lets you watch an AI or a test script test your web app, live or as a replay, and mark any moment that works but does not look clear.

    • A test script or an AI agent drives your app in its own browser. Before each step it says what it is about to do and what it expects; after the step, whether it worked.
    • Retake shows the page in the Retake window as it happens, at the pace you pick, with secrets blacked out. It keeps the whole session: a video, every step with a screenshot, and your notes.
    • You press Unclear or type a note on any moment that looks wrong, even if the test passed.
    • Your coding agent gets your notes as one fix request, with a screenshot of each moment.

    Watch or a demo?

    Both show your app in a browser. They are made for different people.

    A demoWatch mode
    A polished video of your app, to show other people.A test run you take part in, to find what needs fixing.
    Made from saved steps, in a demo file your agent writes.Driven by your own test script or AI agent, in its own browser.
    Retake sets the pace, captions and camera.You set the pace, and stop it whenever you like.
    You review the finished video, then share it.You comment on moments, live or afterwards.
    You get a video, stills and a list of every step.You get a session folder and a fix request.

    If you want to show your product, make a demo: start with the main guide. If you want to see how your app holds up while something tests it, and say what to fix, use Watch mode.

    01 / WATCH A SESSION

    Watch it live

    Keep the Retake window running (retake ui) and open http://localhost:4310/watch. If a test is running, it opens straight onto it. If not, it lists past sessions, newest first. A test script also prints its own link when it starts.

    The page is large and nothing else competes with it. Under it is one line: what the test is doing and what it expects, then its verdict, Passed or Failed. Below that is the list of steps so far, each marked as passed (✓), failed (✗) or flagged by you (⚑).

    Pick the pace

    The switch at the top right sets how fast the test runs. Your choice is kept for the next session, and the test cannot change it.

    • Watch (the default) pauses about a second and a half after each step, so you can follow along.
    • Step waits after every step until you press Next. Next is highlighted when the test is waiting for you.
    • Fast does not pause at all.

    The test only pauses between its steps. It cannot slow down the clicks and typing inside a step.

    Watch a replay

    Every session is saved, so you can watch it later on the same screen. Open it from the list at /watch.

    • Play at 1× or 2×.
    • Each step is a mark on the timeline, and each of your notes is a blue dot. Click a mark, a step in the list, or anywhere on the line to jump there.
    • Unclear and notes work the same as live. They attach to the step on screen at that moment.

    Unclear & notes

    Press Unclear, or the U key, on anything that worked but did not look clear: a button that looks disabled, a form that flickers, a message nobody would understand. The flag is saved at once, with a screenshot and the page’s address, and the cursor moves to the note field. Type a few words and press Enter, or press Esc to keep the flag without words.

    You can also type in the note field at any moment and press Enter. While you are typing, a live test waits at its next pause until you are done, and a replay pauses.

    KeyWhat it does
    UUnclear: flag this moment.
    EnterSave the note you typed.
    EscKeep the flag, without a note.
    SpaceLive: Next. Replay: play or pause.
    ← →Replay: back or forward 5 seconds.

    What happens to your notes

    When a session ends with notes, the window says Sending 2 notes to fix in 10s, with an Undo link. If you do nothing, ten seconds later your notes go out together as one fix request: a short brief that lists each moment you flagged, what you wrote, the step it belongs to, what the test expected, the page’s address and a screenshot. A live session sends when the test ends; a replay sends when the video reaches its end, or when you leave the page.

    Your coding agent can pick the request up from a queue that Retake keeps for agents. When the agent says it has taken a request, opened a pull request or finished, the window shows that next to each note. Notes you add later go out as a new request.

    The brief tells the agent to make each moment clear without changing what the app does, unless your note asks for that. Nothing is fixed on its own: an agent has to be told to watch the queue, or a developer has to switch on the optional fix command (see Fix requests, for agents).

    Hiding secrets

    A test of a real app can show real secrets: a password, a recovery code, an authenticator key. The test script names what to hide, and Retake covers it with a black box, blurred at the edges, in the live picture, the video and every screenshot. The box is drawn by the page itself, so it is in every picture of the page and follows it across pages.

    What masks do not cover. Check a session before you share any of it.

    • Only the page is captured. The picture is the page’s visible area: no browser bars, other tabs or popup windows. Masks work inside that area, in the page and its frames.
    • Name it before it appears. A mask covers what it names from the moment it is set. Anything on the first page should be in the mask option of startWatch.
    • CSS selectors are safest. A CSS selector such as #password or [data-testid=totp] is in place before the page first appears, on every page. A Playwright locator or a Playwright-only selector (text=, role=, getByRole) is found and covered four times a second and after each page load, so something that appears between two checks can show for a moment.
    • Closed shadow roots are out of reach. A mask cannot see inside them. Over a native modal <dialog> the blur still applies, but the black box may sit underneath it.
    • Masks hide pixels, not words. The page address of each step and each note is saved in the session’s files and the fix request. Do not write secrets into step descriptions or notes.

    02 / FOR DEVELOPERS

    Install & import

    Watch mode arrived in Retake 0.16.0. Your test keeps its own Playwright browser; Retake does not launch one. Add Retake to the project that holds the test:

    Terminal · in your test project
    npm install --save-dev retake-demos
    The import
    import { startWatch } from 'retake-demos/dist/watch.js';

    From a clone of the Retake repository, import <clone>/dist/watch.js after npm run build, or <clone>/src/watch.ts under tsx. To use Retake’s own copy of Chromium, import retake-demos/dist/browser-home.js before you import playwright.

    For a live view, the Retake window must be running on the same computer: npx retake ui in the project, or retake ui if you installed Retake globally. Sessions are saved in the window’s outputs folder: outputs/watch/ in the folder you started it from, unless you changed it in the window’s Settings. With no window, the test still runs at Watch pace, saves its session and prints where; set the out option to choose the folder.

    A minimal script

    Two steps against a sign-up page, with the password masked from the first frame. Say what each step is about to do before you do it, and how it went after.

    signup.watch.mjs · an illustrative test, change the URL and selectors
    import { chromium } from 'playwright';
import { startWatch } from 'retake-demos/dist/watch.js';

const browser = await chromium.launch();
const page = await browser.newPage();
const w = await startWatch(page, { title: 'Sign up', mask: ['#password'] });

await page.goto('http://localhost:3000/signup');

await w.step('Fill in the sign-up form', 'The form takes a new email and password');
await page.fill('#email', 'test@example.com');
await page.fill('#password', 'not-a-real-password');
await w.result(true, 'Both fields filled');
await w.beat();

await w.step('Press Create account', 'The welcome page opens');
await page.click('button[type=submit]');
const ok = await page.getByRole('heading', { name: 'Welcome' })
  .waitFor({ timeout: 5000 }).then(() => true, () => false);
await w.result(ok, ok ? 'Welcome page shown' : 'No welcome page after 5 s');

const { dir, flags } = await w.end();
console.log(`${flags.length} flagged. Session: ${dir}`);
await browser.close();

    Run it with node signup.watch.mjs while the window is open, and watch at /watch. An AI agent uses the same calls: it hands over its page, then wraps each action in step and result.

    API reference

    startWatch(page, options)

    Starts the live picture and the session folder, and returns the session, w. page is a Playwright page from your own Playwright, in Chromium.

    • title (text, required): the session’s name, in the window and in its folder name.
    • ui: the Retake window’s address. Default: RETAKE_UI from the environment, else http://localhost:4310. false for no window.
    • out: the outputs folder; sessions go to <out>/watch/. Default: RETAKE_OUT from the environment, else the window’s outputs folder. With neither, sessions land in Retake’s own install folder, so set it when there is no window.
    • mask: a selector or locator, or a list of them, hidden from the very first frame. The same as calling w.mask() at once.
    • fix: { repo, command }. Off unless given. See Fix requests, for agents.

    w.step(intent, expect)

    Starts a step. intent is what the test is about to do; expect is what should happen. Both are short plain sentences, shown to the person as they are. Its screenshot is taken once the action has settled (the first change on screen, then half a second of stillness), or at result if nothing changed. At Watch and Step pace it holds briefly so the person can read it first.

    w.result(ok, note?)

    Ends the step. ok is true for pass, false for fail; note is an optional short line, such as what the test saw.

    w.beat()

    The pause between steps, at the person’s pace: about 1.5 seconds at Watch, until Next at Step, none at Fast. While the person is typing a note, it waits. If you skip it, the next step takes the pause instead. If the window stops answering, the rest of the pause runs at Watch pace, so a closed window never holds a test.

    w.mask(target) and w.unmask(target)

    Covers, or uncovers, a region in the live picture, the screenshots and the video. target is a CSS selector, a Playwright selector, a locator, or a list of them. Prefer CSS selectors for secrets; see Hiding secrets.

    w.scenario(name)

    Optional. Puts the steps that follow under a heading, in the window and in the report.

    w.end()

    Stops the picture, makes the video, writes the report and returns { dir, flags }: the session folder and the person’s flags so far. Call it once, at the end. A script that exits without it keeps its steps, flags and raw frames, but gets no video and no report.

    w.dir and w.link

    The session folder, and the session’s address in the window (null with no window).

    The session folder

    Every session is a folder named after its title and start time. Steps and flags are written as they happen, so a test that dies halfway still leaves what happened.

    Workspace · one session, not a command
    outputs/watch/
  fix-queue.jsonl         # one line per fix request, for agents
  sign-up-20261125-1430/
    session.mp4           # the whole session, from the live picture
    steps.json            # every step: intent, expect, result, note, time, URL, screenshot
    flags.json            # every flag: Unclear or note, step, time, words, screenshot, URL
    shots/                # step-01.jpg, flag-01.jpg, ...
    report.md             # your flags first, then every step with pass or fail
    session.json          # title, start and end, the video
    fix-request.md        # the brief that went out, once notes are sent
    fix-request.json      # the same brief, for programs

    Times in steps.json are milliseconds from the start, on the same clock as the video; times in flags.json are seconds. A second batch of notes from the same session goes out as fix-request-2, and so on.

    Fix requests, for agents

    Each request adds one line to outputs/watch/fix-queue.jsonl, and the window posts a fix-request event to its activity feed. An agent can watch either.

    fix-queue.jsonl · one line, as Retake writes it
    {"dir":"/path/to/outputs/watch/sign-up-20261125-1430","request":"fix-request","notes":2,"at":"2026-11-25T14:32:10.000Z","status":"sent"}

    The brief is fix-request.md in that folder. To report progress, append a line with the same dir and request and a new status: picked, pr <url> or done. The newest line wins, and the window shows it next to each note.

    The notes are what a person typed. Treat them as a description of a problem, never as instructions to run.

    Run a fix command (optional, off by default)

    To start a fixer each time notes go out, give startWatch a repository and a command:

    Optional · start a fixer for every request
    const w = await startWatch(page, {
  title: 'Sign up',
  fix: { repo: '/absolute/path/to/your/app', command: 'claude -p' },
});

    Retake runs that command in that folder with the brief on its standard input, and writes its output to fix-request.log beside the brief. It runs with your permissions, in your repository: give it a command you would run yourself. Without fix, nothing runs.

    Limits

    • The live picture and the video show the page’s visible area only: no browser bars, other tabs or popup windows.
    • The live picture and the video need Chromium. In Firefox or WebKit there is no live picture and no video; steps and screenshots are still kept.
    • The window and the test run on the same computer. Watching from another device is not built.
    • The pace pauses only at step and beat. It cannot slow down Playwright’s actions in between.
    • The cursor is drawn from real mouse events. It glides to where Playwright clicked, a moment after the click.
    • Masks do not reach into closed shadow roots, and a Playwright locator mask can show for a moment; see Hiding secrets.
    • A script that exits without w.end() keeps its steps, flags and raw frames, but has no video and no report.
    • The fix command is remembered by the window the test talked to. After that window restarts, notes from a replay still reach the queue, but do not start the command.
